Why PAD measures area and efficiency

Calculating area and building efficiency is the primary goal of the design team for Patry Group buildings. Every unit in PAD shows its net area, its gross area and its efficiency, and they update as you describe changes.

Efficiency = Usable Floor Area (UFA) ÷ Gross Floor Area (GFA)

In PAD, the keyplan shows the units on both sides of a 1.6 m corridor, each unit's gross area includes half of that corridor, and a building's efficiency is its total net area divided by its total gross area. Stairs, elevators and shafts are not drawn yet, so a finished building will come in lower than the figure shown.

1Optimizing units

Net-to-gross area efficiency is the ratio of Usable Floor Area (UFA) to Gross Floor Area (GFA), expressed as a percentage. It measures the spatial efficiency of a layout. It shows how much of the building's total constructed footprint becomes functional, rentable or livable tenant space, and how much is structural loss.

2Usable Floor Area (UFA)

Definition
The actual space inside the finished surfaces of individual suites or leasable commercial bays, measured wall to wall.
Measurement
From the faces of finished walls and the finished faces of interior partitions.
Inclusions
Habitable rooms, internal partitions, closets, and in-suite mechanical and laundry footprints.
Exclusions (loss factors)
Vertical service shafts, elevators, fire stairwells, shared public corridors, garbage chutes, main electrical and mechanical rooms, and management offices. These are the core loss of the building.

3Gross Floor Area (GFA)

Definition
In line with standard Ontario municipal zoning bylaws, GFA is measured to the exterior face of the exterior perimeter walls of the building, across all floors.
Measurement
From the centre line of the demising wall to the centre line of the demising wall. On the exterior wall, to the outside of the framing studs. On the corridor side, to the corridor side of the stud on the interior wall.
Inclusions
All enclosed levels above grade, including corridors, mechanical penthouses (depending on local zoning exclusions), and shared amenity core spaces.
Exclusions
Unenclosed exterior balconies, patios, roof terraces, tuck-under garages, and standard parking and below-grade storage structures (unless local zoning specifically counts underground footprints).

4Optimizing buildings

Total Net Rentable Area (NRA)
The sum of UFA multiplied by the number of units. Excludes patios and balconies.
Residential Gross Area (RGA)
The total building envelope minus all retail spaces. Includes tuck-under parking. Excludes patios and balconies.
Building efficiency
NRA divided by RGA. An efficiency of 80% or higher is the baseline, unless Patry Group sets other targets during pre-application or feasibility analysis.

5Optimizing for the Ontario framework

To suit a rental development framework, this approach avoids ambiguous terms like "net rentable", which belongs in commercial leasing, and anchors everything on one clear ratio.

  • Zoning compliance alignment. Because the gross measure follows exterior wall measurements, designers work within standard municipal zoning definitions of GFA.
  • OBC compatibility. It respects the Ontario Building Code's spatial separation and fire-compartment boundaries, without mixing structural footprint calculations with commercial leasing metrics such as BOMA, or Tarion standards, which do not apply cleanly to multi-family residential rental developments.
  • Target benchmarking. A clear formula lets the designer set baseline efficiency expectations. A multi-family residential building should reach a minimum of 80% to 85% UFA-to-GFA ratio. That gives the design team an objective measure for optimizing hallway lengths, core layouts and structural grid placement.
